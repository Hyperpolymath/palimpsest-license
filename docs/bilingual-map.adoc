=== 1. Translation Framework

==== 1.1 Legal Status of Language Versions

*Dutch Version (Nederlandse Versie):* - *Legal Status:* Legally binding
in the Kingdom of the Netherlands - *Authority:* Primary authentic text
for Dutch courts - *Legal Basis:* Boek 6 van het Burgerlijk Wetboek
(Book 6, Dutch Civil Code) - *Register:* Formal legal Dutch (juridisch
Nederlands) - *Spelling:* Nederlandse spelling volgens Spelling 2015

*English Version:* - *Legal Status:* Informative, internationally
accessible version - *Authority:* Not legally binding in Netherlands
unless parties explicitly agree - *Legal Tradition:* British Common Law
terminology (for international comprehension) - *Register:* Formal legal
English - *Spelling:* British English (colour, licence, organisation,
not color, license, organization) - *Grammar:* British conventions
(quotation marks, date formats, etc.)

==== 1.2 Translation Methodology

*Functional Equivalence Approach:* - Translates legal *effect* and
*intent*, not merely words - Identifies equivalent legal concepts
between Dutch civil law and British common law systems - Explicitly
acknowledges where no direct equivalent exists - Preserves structural
parallelism (clause numbering, headings, formatting)

*Harmonised EU Terminology:* - Uses standardised EU multilingual legal
terminology where applicable - References EUR-Lex glossary for copyright
and IP terms - Consistent with EU Directives (InfoSoc Directive
2001/29/EC, DSM Directive 2019/790)

*Cultural and Ethical Sensitivity:* - Preserves symbolic and emotional
depth of Palimpsest concepts - Maintains accessibility and
plain-language orientation - Respects diaspora narratives, trauma
stories, and cultural heritage terminology

==== 1.3 Precedence Rules

*Conflict Resolution:*

In the event of discrepancy between language versions:

[width="100%",cols="32%,40%,28%",options="header",]
|===
|*Jurisdiction* |*Prevailing Version* |*Legal Basis*
|*Netherlands* |Dutch version prevails |Primary legal text under Dutch

|*Scotland* |English version used; Dutch version for interpretation
|Hague Convention 2005; Scots law proceedings

|*Other jurisdictions* |English version (or local translation if
available) |International accessibility

|*Arbitration (UNCITRAL)* |Both versions considered; Dutch version
authoritative for Dutch law interpretation |Arbitration rules allow
reference to both
|===

=== 4. Translation Rationale

==== 4.1 Conceptual Translation Challenges

===== 4.1.1 "`Verflauwing`" → "`Flattening`"

*Challenge:* - Dutch _verflauwing_ derives from _verflauwen_ ("`to
fade`", "`to weaken`", "`to lose intensity`") - No single English legal
term captures this cultural/aesthetic concept

*Options Considered:* 1. *Dilution* — too commercial (trademark law
connotations) 2. *Trivialisation* — too judgemental, lacks metaphorical
depth 3. *Reductionism* — too academic, not accessible 4. *Flattening* —
metaphor for loss of dimensionality

*Chosen Solution:* *"`Flattening`"* - *Rationale*: Resonates with
"`layered`" metaphor (palimpsest as layers); accessible to creative
communities; captures loss of depth without legal jargon - *Cultural
fit*: Used in digital humanities (flattening of texts), art criticism
(flattening of meaning) - *Consistency*: Used throughout Clause 0, 2, 8

===== 4.1.2 "`Emotionele afstamming`" → "`Emotional lineage`"

*Challenge:* - Dutch _afstamming_ = "`descent`", "`ancestry`",
"`lineage`" (biological/genealogical) - Applying biological metaphor to
creative works requires care

*Options Considered:* 1. *Emotional descent* — awkward phrasing 2.
*Emotional ancestry* — too literal (suggests dead ancestors) 3.
*Emotional lineage* — genealogical metaphor, cultural resonance 4.
*Emotional provenance* — too art-market focused

*Chosen Solution:* *"`Emotional lineage`"* - *Rationale*: "`Lineage`"
evokes cultural heritage, oral traditions, narrative transmission -
*Cultural sensitivity*: Respectful of diaspora narratives, Indigenous
knowledge systems - *Accessibility*: Widely understood; used in cultural
studies, anthropology - *Consistency*: Parallel with "`Lineage
Statement`" (Clause 3.2), "`Synthetic Lineage Tag`" (Clause 6.3)

===== 4.1.3 "`Symbolische erkenning`" → "`Symbolic attribution`"

*Challenge:* - Dutch _erkenning_ broader than English "`attribution`"
(includes recognition, acknowledgement, validation) - Copyright law uses
"`attribution`"; Palimpsest concept extends beyond legal credit

*Options Considered:* 1. *Symbolic recognition* — loses legal grounding
2. *Symbolic credit* — too informal 3. *Symbolic acknowledgement* —
passive, lacks agency 4. *Symbolic attribution* — legal term + cultural
modifier

*Chosen Solution:* *"`Symbolic attribution`"* - *Rationale*: Combines
standard copyright term ("`attribution`") with Palimpsest modifier
("`symbolic`") - *Legal clarity*: "`Attribution`" understood in CC
licenses, Berne Convention - *Cultural depth*: "`Symbolic`" signals
beyond-legal recognition (cultural context, emotional debt) -
*Consistency*: Used throughout Clause 1, 2, 6

==== 4.2 Register and Formality Decisions

===== 4.2.1 Formal Legal Language vs Accessibility

*Tension:* - Legal precision requires formal register (juridical
terminology) - Palimpsest ethos prioritises accessibility (plain
language, creative communities)

*Translation Approach:* - *Core legal clauses* (Clauses 4, 9): Use
standard legal terms (e.g. "`irrevocable`", "`jurisdiction`",
"`termination`") - *Cultural/ethical clauses* (Clauses 0, 1, 2, 8):
Balance legal terms with accessible language (e.g. "`flattening`" not
"`dilution`") - *Definitions* (Clause Definitions): Provide
plain-language explanations with legal anchors

*Example:* - *Clause 9.2* (Dutch): _Symbolische erkenning ontbreekt_ -
*Literal translation*: "`Symbolic recognition is lacking`" - *Legal
translation*: "`Attribution failure`" - *Chosen*: "`Attribution
failure`" — combines legal precision ("`attribution`") with
accessibility ("`failure`" clearer than "`absence`" or "`deficiency`")

===== 4.2.2 British English vs American English

*Policy:* - *Spelling*: British English (colour, licence, organisation)
- *Grammar*: British conventions (quotation marks, date formats) -
*Legal terminology*: British legal terms where applicable

*Rationale:* - *Jurisdiction*: Enforcement venue is Scotland (British
jurisdiction) - *International accessibility*: British English more
neutral internationally than American English - *EU alignment*:
Harmonised with EU English (British-style)

*Register Mismatches:*

* *Too informal*: "`The license lets you remix stuff`" → *Correct*:
"`The license permits remixing`"
* *Too archaic*: "`Hereinafter the licensor doth grant`" → *Correct*:
"`The licensor grants`"
* *Inconsistent formality*: Mixing "`shall`" and "`must`" → *Correct*:
Use "`must`" throughout (modern British legal English)

*Cultural Insensitivity:*

* *Exoticising*: "`native knowledge`" → *Correct*: "`Indigenous
knowledge`" (capitalised)
* *Euphemising trauma*: "`difficult stories`" → *Correct*: "`trauma
narratives`"
* *Othering language*: "`their culture`" → *Correct*: Specify community
or use neutral phrasing

==== 6.4 Version Control and Change Management

===== 6.4.1 Bilingual Versioning Protocol

*Simultaneous Versioning:* - Dutch and English versions *always have
same version number* (e.g. v0.3, v0.4) - Any amendment to one version
requires corresponding amendment to other - Changelog documents changes
in both versions

*Amendment Process:* 1. *Proposal* in either language (submitted to
Council) 2. *Translation* if proposal in one language only 3. *Bilingual
review* of proposed amendment 4. *Council vote* on both versions
simultaneously 5. *Publication* of both versions with same version
number and date

===== 6.4.2 Changelog Documentation

*Required Information:* - Version number (e.g. v0.3 → v0.4) - Date of
adoption - *Bilingual description* of changes (Dutch and English) -
Clause(s) affected - Rationale for change - Impact on existing licensees

*Example:*

[source,markdown]
----
## Version 0.4 (November 2025)

### Clause 1.2 — Non-Interpretive Systems (NEW)
- **Nederlands**: Toegevoegd: Expliciete toestemming vereist voor gebruik in niet-interpretatieve AI-systemen
- **English**: Added: Explicit consent required for use in non-interpretive AI systems

==== 7.3 Translation Workflow for Palimpsest

===== 7.3.1 Pre-Translation Phase

*Step 1: Familiarisation* 1. Read existing license versions (v0.3 Dutch
and English) 2. Study this bilingual map document 3. Review legal
lexicon (`+legal/translations/dutch-english-legal-lexicon.md+`) 4.
Understand Palimpsest ethos (read `+CLAUDE.md+`, `+README.md+`,
governance documents)

*Step 2: Terminology Extraction* 1. Identify all defined terms
(capitalised terms, technical terms) 2. Extract new terms not in
existing lexicon 3. Research legal equivalents (EUR-Lex, WIPO Pearl,
legal dictionaries) 4. Draft provisional translations for new terms

*Step 3: Reference Materials* 1. Set up translation memory (TM) with
existing v0.3 translations 2. Load terminology database (approved term
pairs) 3. Prepare style guide (British English, formal register) 4.
Gather parallel texts (EU Directives, CC licenses, GPL)

===== 7.3.2 Translation Phase

*Step 1: Clause-by-Clause Translation* 1. Translate one clause at a time
(maintain structure) 2. Use approved terminology from database 3. Apply
functional equivalence (legal effect, not literal words) 4. Flag
untranslatable concepts for explanatory notes

*Step 2: Self-Revision* 1. Read translation without source text (fluency
check) 2. Compare with source text (accuracy check) 3. Verify
terminology consistency (same term always translated identically) 4.
Check cross-references (clause numbers, file paths)

*Step 3: Alignment Verification* 1. Create side-by-side comparison
(Dutch | English) 2. Verify clause numbering matches 3. Check list
formatting (bullets, numbering) 4. Ensure capitalisation consistent
(defined terms)

===== 7.3.3 Post-Translation Phase

*Step 1: Peer Review* 1. Second translator reviews (legal accuracy) 2.
Legal expert reviews (functional equivalence) 3. Cultural sensitivity
review (diaspora communities, accessibility advocates)

*Step 2: Bilingual Map Update* 1. Add new clauses to bilingual map
(clause-by-clause alignment) 2. Document translation rationale for key
terms 3. Update terminology database with new terms 4. Note any
non-equivalent concepts (explanatory notes)

*Step 3: Council Review and Approval* 1. Submit to Palimpsest
Stewardship Council 2. Respond to queries and revisions 3. Finalise both
versions simultaneously 4. Publish with version control (Git)

==== 7.4 Quality Control Checklist for Translators

===== 7.4.1 Linguistic Quality

* [ ] *Spelling*: British English (colour, licence, organisation)
* [ ] *Grammar*: British conventions (quotation marks, punctuation)
* [ ] *Register*: Formal legal language (consistent throughout)
* [ ] *Terminology*: Approved terms from database
* [ ] *Consistency*: Same term always translated identically
* [ ] *Fluency*: Reads naturally in target language (not stilted)

===== 7.4.2 Legal Accuracy

* [ ] *Functional equivalence*: Legal effect identical in both versions
* [ ] *Rights and obligations*: Same in Dutch and English
* [ ] *Prohibitions*: Aligned (what’s prohibited in Dutch is prohibited
in English)
* [ ] *Procedures*: Identical steps (e.g. dispute resolution)
* [ ] *Defined terms*: Capitalised and used consistently

===== 7.4.3 Structural Alignment

* [ ] *Clause numbering*: Identical (1.1, 1.2, etc.)
* [ ] *Headings*: Parallel hierarchy (H2, H3 levels)
* [ ] *Lists*: Same formatting (bullets, numbering)
* [ ] *Cross-references*: Accurate (clause numbers, file paths)
* [ ] *Emphasis*: Consistent (bold, italics for same terms)

===== 7.4.4 Cultural Sensitivity

* [ ] *Respectful language*: Diaspora, trauma, Indigenous communities
* [ ] *No exoticisation*: Avoid othering language
* [ ] *Accessibility*: Plain language where possible
* [ ] *Inclusive examples*: Diverse communities represented
